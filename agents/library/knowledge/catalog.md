# Riverside Public Library catalog

## Book suggestions and recommendations
The library is happy to suggest something to read. It holds fiction and non-fiction for adults, and books for children and young readers. Ask for a genre and the information line will suggest a few titles: mystery, science fiction, fantasy, historical fiction, romance and classics, non-fiction, or children's and young adult books. The staff picks this month are The Martian, The Thursday Murder Club, Pride and Prejudice, and Charlotte's Web.
People often ask: can you suggest a book, recommend something to read, what should I read next, what is a good book, what is popular, what are the staff picks, what books do you have.

## Mystery books
Mystery and detective books on the shelf: The Hound of the Baskervilles by Arthur Conan Doyle, a Sherlock Holmes case about a ghostly dog on the moors. And Then There Were None by Agatha Christie, where ten strangers are stranded on an island with a killer. The Thursday Murder Club by Richard Osman, about four retirees who solve murders for fun.
People often ask: suggest a mystery, recommend a detective story, a whodunit, a crime novel, a murder mystery, something like Sherlock Holmes or Agatha Christie.

## Science fiction books
Science fiction books on the shelf: The Martian by Andy Weir, an astronaut stranded alone on Mars. Dune by Frank Herbert, an epic about a young heir on a desert planet. Project Hail Mary by Andy Weir, a man who wakes up alone on a spaceship and has to save the Earth.
People often ask: suggest a science fiction book, recommend a sci-fi novel, space stories, books about space, astronauts, aliens, the future.

## Fantasy books
Fantasy books on the shelf: The Hobbit by J. R. R. Tolkien, a homebody hobbit who goes on a quest with dwarves to win treasure from a dragon. A Wizard of Earthsea by Ursula K. Le Guin, about a young wizard hunting a shadow he set free. Piranesi by Susanna Clarke, a strange and beautiful mystery set in a house of endless halls.
People often ask: suggest a fantasy book, recommend a fantasy novel, wizards, magic, dragons, quests, something like Lord of the Rings or Harry Potter.

## Historical fiction books
Historical fiction books on the shelf: All the Light We Cannot See by Anthony Doerr, about a blind French girl and a German boy during the Second World War. The Book Thief by Markus Zusak, about a girl who steals books in Nazi Germany, narrated by Death.
People often ask: suggest historical fiction, recommend a book set in the past, war stories, books about the Second World War, history novels.

## Romance and classics books
Romance and classic books on the shelf: Pride and Prejudice by Jane Austen, the witty classic about Elizabeth Bennet and Mr Darcy. Beach Read by Emily Henry, a modern romance about two rival writers who are neighbors for a summer.
People often ask: suggest a romance, recommend a love story, a classic novel, a romantic comedy, a light read, a feel-good book.

## Non-fiction books
Non-fiction books on the shelf: Sapiens by Yuval Noah Harari, a short history of humankind. Educated by Tara Westover, a memoir about growing up without school and teaching herself. Atomic Habits by James Clear, a practical guide to building good habits.
People often ask: suggest a non-fiction book, recommend a memoir, a history book, a self-help book, a book about habits, a true story, something to learn from.

## Children's and young adult books
Children's and young adult books on the shelf: Charlotte's Web by E. B. White, the story of a pig and the spider who saves him. Matilda by Roald Dahl, about a brilliant girl who stands up to her awful headmistress. The Hunger Games by Suzanne Collins, a young adult thriller about a girl who takes her sister's place in a deadly contest.
People often ask: suggest a book for kids, something for my child, children's books, a book for my son or daughter, a book for a six, eight or ten year old, young readers, teenagers, a bedtime story, young adult.

## Where to find books
Fiction is on the ground floor, arranged by genre. Non-fiction is on the first floor. The children's room, with the young adult shelves, is at the back of the ground floor. Staff can walk you to a shelf.
People often ask: where is the fiction section, where can I find non-fiction, where are the children's books, which floor, how do I find a book on the shelf.

## Is a particular book available
The information line knows about the titles on the staff picks and genre shelves above. It cannot see which copies are on the shelf right now, and it does not know every title in the building. The front desk can check whether a specific book is in and can put a hold on one that is out.
People often ask: do you have a certain title, is this book available, is it in stock, can I borrow a specific book, can you check a book, can I reserve a book.
